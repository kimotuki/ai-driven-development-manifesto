# AI-Driven Development Practice Guide

These are the current, concrete actions for putting the principles of the [AI-Driven Development Manifesto](./MANIFESTO.en.md) into practice.
Because the landscape around AI changes every few months, this guide is updated continuously, separately from the manifesto itself.

Last updated: 2026-10-11

## [1] What needs to be done tactically in the near term

- Adopting AI development tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, etc.)
- Adopting MCP
- Establishing rules and context for AI agents
- Formulating security guidelines
- Building tests, linting, static analysis, and security scanning into CI/CD, so that whatever can be verified mechanically is left to mechanisms
- Participating in external communities

## [2] What needs to be done early

- Improving skills in using AI development tools
- Shifting to AI-readable information (specifications, design documents, etc.)
- Building mechanisms that allow AI agents to access the company's business information (setting up local MCP)
- Establishing guidelines for AI agents and building a platform for AI-driven development
- Separating the points where humans decide (requirements, architecture, risk acceptance, release decisions) from the points that mechanisms verify
- Recording deliverables that AI generates or changes (instructions, context, evidence, adoption decision)
- Redesigning an AI-driven development process suited to the company
- Fostering an internal community

## [3] What should be prepared strategically to secure a competitive advantage

- Establishing an organization to promote AI-driven development and continuously updating knowledge
- Mechanisms, such as policy as code, that automatically verify and constrain the actions of AI agents
- Building a security foundation for AI agents (able to detect, stop, and recover from malfunctions)
- Documenting the division of responsibility and approval rules for deliverables AI is involved in
- Defining new roles for engineers, POs, and others involved in software development, and articulating career models
